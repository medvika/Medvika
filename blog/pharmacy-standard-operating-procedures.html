Pharmacy Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs): Complete Guide

Build consistent pharmacy operations with practical SOPs for inventory, dispensing, billing, customer service, stock management and staff training.

Standard Operating Procedures, commonly called SOPs, are written instructions that explain how routine pharmacy activities should be performed.

Good SOPs reduce confusion, improve consistency and make it easier to train staff, monitor performance and correct repeated operational problems.

This guide explains the key SOPs a retail pharmacy should consider and provides practical frameworks that can be adapted to the pharmacy's size, software, staffing pattern and applicable requirements.

01

What Are Pharmacy SOPs?

A pharmacy SOP is a controlled document that describes who should perform a task, when it should be performed, how it should be completed and what records should be kept.

A useful SOP should answer five questions

What is the task? Who is responsible? When should it happen? How should it be done? What evidence or record should remain?

02

Why Every Pharmacy Needs SOPs

Consistency

Routine tasks are completed in the same approved way.

Training

New staff can learn responsibilities through structured guidance.

Risk Control

Important checks are less likely to be forgotten.

Improvement

Management can audit the process and measure performance.

  • Clarify individual responsibilities.
  • Reduce dependence on verbal instructions.
  • Improve stock and expiry control.
  • Support safer and more consistent dispensing workflows.
  • Improve billing, cash and documentation controls.
  • Make pharmacy audits easier.
03

Receiving Medicines SOP

  1. Accept deliveries only from approved suppliers.
  2. Compare the supplier invoice with the purchase order.
  3. Check product name, manufacturer, pack size and quantity.
  4. Verify batch number and expiry date.
  5. Check packaging for damage, leakage or tampering.
  6. Separate cold-chain products immediately.
  7. Record shortages, excess supply or substitutions.
  8. Enter the purchase accurately into the software.
  9. Place stock correctly using FEFO.
  10. File invoices and receiving records.
04

Inventory Management SOP

  • Set minimum, maximum and reorder levels.
  • Review fast-moving, slow-moving and non-moving reports.
  • Use ABC analysis for high-value stock.
  • Conduct regular physical verification.
  • Investigate negative stock and unexplained adjustments.
  • Restrict manual stock changes to authorized users.
  • Record the reason for every adjustment.
  • Stop ordering products with low demand or excessive stock.

See the Pharmacy Inventory Management Guide for a detailed framework.

05

FEFO Stock Rotation SOP

  1. Check expiry dates during receiving.
  2. Place earlier-expiry batches at the front.
  3. Keep later-expiry batches behind them.
  4. Select the correct batch during billing.
  5. Review short-dated stock every month.
  6. Stop repeat ordering when enough stock exists.
  7. Move, return or exchange risky stock early.

06

Cold Chain Storage SOP

  • Transfer cold-chain products to the refrigerator immediately after receipt.
  • Maintain the storage range specified for the product.
  • Record temperature at defined intervals.
  • Avoid overloading the refrigerator.
  • Do not store food or unrelated items with medicines.
  • Document temperature excursions.
  • Maintain a backup plan for power failure or equipment breakdown.
07

Prescription Handling and Dispensing SOP

  1. Receive and review the prescription.
  2. Check required patient and prescriber details.
  3. Assess legibility and completeness.
  4. Clarify uncertainties appropriately.
  5. Select the correct medicine, strength, dosage form and quantity.
  6. Check batch and expiry date.
  7. Complete billing and required documentation.
  8. Perform a final check before handover.
  9. Provide appropriate usage and storage guidance within professional scope.
  10. Maintain confidentiality.
08

Billing and Cash Handling SOP

  • Bill every sale through the approved system.
  • Select the correct product, batch, quantity and price.
  • Control manual discounts and price overrides.
  • Document cancellations and sales returns.
  • Reconcile cash, card, UPI and other payments daily.
  • Restrict access to refunds and bill deletion.
  • Maintain opening and closing cash records.
09

Customer Service SOP

  • Greet and acknowledge customers promptly.
  • Listen carefully and confirm the request.
  • Communicate availability honestly.
  • Escalate professional questions to the pharmacist.
  • Provide realistic pickup or delivery timelines.
  • Protect customer information.
  • Record and resolve complaints.
10

Medicine Returns SOP

  1. Identify whether the return is from a customer or to a supplier.
  2. Check invoice, product, batch, expiry and packaging condition.
  3. Confirm eligibility under policy.
  4. Keep returned stock separated until a decision is made.
  5. Complete the approved return or credit-note process.
  6. Adjust stock only through authorized entries.
  7. Maintain supporting documentation.
11

Expired and Damaged Medicine SOP

  • Remove expired and damaged stock from saleable inventory immediately.
  • Place it in a clearly marked restricted area.
  • Record product, batch, quantity, value and reason.
  • Check supplier return eligibility.
  • Complete authorized stock adjustment.
  • Maintain disposal or handover records.
  • Investigate repeated expiry or damage.
12

Daily Pharmacy Opening Checklist

  • Check cleanliness and safety.
  • Confirm refrigerator and storage conditions.
  • Start the billing system and required devices.
  • Verify opening cash where applicable.
  • Review pending orders and deliveries.
  • Check essential stock-outs.
  • Confirm staff attendance and duty allocation.
13

Daily Pharmacy Closing Checklist

  • Complete pending billing and documentation.
  • Reconcile cash and digital payments.
  • Review cancelled bills, returns and discounts.
  • Secure cash, records and restricted stock.
  • Check refrigerator power and temperature status.
  • Complete shift handover notes.
  • Close systems and secure the premises.
14

Staff Training SOP

  1. Provide induction training before independent work.
  2. Explain SOPs and reporting structure.
  3. Demonstrate software and inventory processes.
  4. Train staff on FEFO, expiry and storage.
  5. Observe practical performance.
  6. Record training completion.
  7. Provide corrective training after repeated errors.
  8. Review training needs during audits.
15

Common SOP Mistakes to Avoid

  • Copying generic SOPs without adapting them.
  • Writing procedures staff cannot realistically follow.
  • Using unclear language or undefined responsibilities.
  • Failing to train staff after issuing an SOP.
  • Keeping outdated versions in circulation.
  • Not recording evidence of completed tasks.
  • Creating SOPs but never auditing compliance.
  • Changing a process without updating the SOP.

16

Simple Pharmacy SOP Template

  1. SOP title: Clear name of the process.
  2. SOP number: Unique document identifier.
  3. Version: Current revision number.
  4. Effective date: Date the SOP becomes active.
  5. Purpose: Why the procedure exists.
  6. Scope: Where and to whom it applies.
  7. Responsibility: Roles accountable for the process.
  8. Procedure: Step-by-step instructions.
  9. Records: Forms, reports or evidence to retain.
  10. Approval: Approving authority.
  11. Review date: Planned revision date.
17

Conclusion

Pharmacy SOPs turn management expectations into clear, repeatable processes. They improve consistency, support training and make operational weaknesses easier to identify and correct.

Start with high-risk and high-frequency processes such as receiving, inventory, FEFO, dispensing, billing, returns and cash handling. Review SOPs regularly and update them whenever workflows change.
