Pharmacy Audit Checklist: Complete Guide for Retail Pharmacies

Review licensing, inventory, expiry control, storage, billing, staff, cash handling and customer service through a structured pharmacy audit.

A pharmacy audit is a structured review of how the pharmacy actually operates. It helps owners identify hidden losses, weak controls, documentation gaps and service problems before they become serious.

An effective audit compares written procedures, software records, physical stock and day-to-day staff practices.

01

Why Pharmacy Audits Matter

Many pharmacy problems develop gradually. Expiry loss, excess stock, billing errors, weak cash control and poor documentation may remain unnoticed until they affect profitability or create compliance risk.

Reduce Risk

Identify operational and documentation gaps early.

Improve Inventory

Find excess, short-dated and unrecorded stock.

Protect Profit

Reduce losses from wastage, leakage and errors.

Strengthen Teams

Clarify responsibilities and accountability.

02

Prepare Before Starting the Audit

  • Choose the audit date and review period.
  • Define whether the audit is full or department-specific.
  • Collect sales, purchase, stock and expiry reports.
  • Keep licenses, invoices, staff records and SOPs available.
  • Select sample transactions for verification.
  • Classify findings as critical, major or minor.

Audit the process, not only the person

Repeated mistakes often indicate a weak system, unclear responsibility or missing training.

03

Review Licensing and Essential Records

  • Check validity and renewal status of applicable pharmacy licenses.
  • Verify pharmacist documents and duty records.
  • Review purchase invoices and supplier details.
  • Check records required for scheduled or controlled medicines where applicable.
  • Verify relevant tax, business and local registrations.
  • Ensure required notices and certificates are displayed or maintained correctly.

Requirements vary by location and business model, so use rules applicable to the specific pharmacy.

04

Inspect Premises, Storage and Housekeeping

  • Check cleanliness of shelves, counters and storage areas.
  • Protect products from heat, moisture and direct sunlight.
  • Verify refrigerator temperature monitoring where required.
  • Separate saleable, returned, damaged and expired stock.
  • Arrange products for easy FEFO rotation.
  • Inspect fire, electrical and emergency access safety.
  • Review pest-control and waste-handling arrangements.
05

Audit Inventory Accuracy and Stock Control

Compare software quantities with physical stock, especially for high-value, fast-moving and sensitive products.

  1. Select representative products.
  2. Count physical stock batch-wise where possible.
  3. Compare quantities with software records.
  4. Investigate shortages, excesses and adjustments.
  5. Document the cause and corrective action.
  • Review fast-, slow- and non-moving reports.
  • Check negative and zero-stock items.
  • Review high-value stock and repeated adjustments.
  • Correct duplicate products and inaccurate pack sizes.
06

Audit Expiry, Damaged and Returned Stock

  • Generate reports for products expiring within 3, 6 and 9 months.
  • Verify earlier-expiry batches are positioned first.
  • Stop repeat ordering for short-dated products.
  • Review supplier return deadlines and pending credit notes.
  • Confirm segregation of expired and damaged stock.
  • Check documentation for stock removal and adjustment.
  • Calculate expiry loss as a percentage of sales or purchases.
07

Review Purchasing and Goods Receiving

  • Base purchases on demand and reorder levels.
  • Review supplier rates, schemes, credit and return terms.
  • Verify invoices against quantities received.
  • Check batch, expiry and pack size during receiving.
  • Review emergency purchases and excess buying.
  • Confirm purchase entries are accurate and timely.
08

Audit Billing and Transaction Documentation

  • Ensure all sales are billed through the approved system.
  • Check invoice numbering and cancellation controls.
  • Review high discounts, price changes and overrides.
  • Check sales returns and credit notes.
  • Verify correct batch selection.
  • Compare daily sales with cash and digital collections.
09

Review Staff Roles, Training and SOPs

  • Verify pharmacist and staff attendance records.
  • Review job roles and reporting responsibilities.
  • Check training for software, FEFO, billing and service.
  • Confirm SOPs are available and understood.
  • Review error reporting and corrective training.
  • Check shift handover and task-completion records.
10

Audit Cash, Payments and Financial Controls

  • Compare cash collections with billing reports.
  • Reconcile card, UPI and other digital payments.
  • Review refunds, voids and cancelled bills.
  • Check opening and closing cash procedures.
  • Verify petty-cash records.
  • Review supplier balances and payment schedules.
  • Separate personal and business transactions.
11

Evaluate Customer Service and Workflow

  • Observe waiting time and queue management.
  • Review product-availability and substitution handling.
  • Check professional communication standards.
  • Review complaint handling and escalation.
  • Check pickup and delivery coordination where offered.
  • Protect customer and prescription information.
  • Identify repeated reasons for lost sales.
12

Audit Software, Access and Data Quality

  • Review user accounts and permission levels.
  • Disable inactive or shared credentials.
  • Check backup and recovery arrangements.
  • Review audit trails for price, stock and bill changes.
  • Correct duplicate or incomplete product records.
  • Verify batch, expiry, manufacturer and pack-size data.
  • Protect customer and business data.
13

Create a Simple Audit Scoring System

Compliant

The process works and evidence is available.

Partially Compliant

The process exists but is inconsistent.

Non-Compliant

The requirement is missing or ineffective.

Not Applicable

The point does not apply to the operation.

Report critical findings separately so they are not hidden by a high overall score.

14

Prepare a Corrective Action Plan

  1. Describe the finding clearly.
  2. Record supporting evidence.
  3. Identify the root cause.
  4. Define the corrective action.
  5. Assign a responsible person.
  6. Set a completion deadline.
  7. Verify closure with evidence.
  8. Review whether the problem repeats.

Prioritize by risk

Address critical safety, legal, inventory and financial issues before cosmetic improvements.

15

Quick Pharmacy Audit Checklist

  • Licenses and required records are current.
  • Pharmacist and staff records are complete.
  • Storage conditions are appropriate.
  • Physical stock matches software.
  • FEFO is followed.
  • Short-dated and damaged stock is controlled.
  • Purchasing is based on demand.
  • Supplier returns and credit notes are tracked.
  • Sales, returns, cancellations and discounts are controlled.
  • Cash and digital payments are reconciled.
  • Staff roles, SOPs and training are documented.
  • Software access and data quality are controlled.
  • Corrective actions are closed on time.
16

Conclusion

A pharmacy audit should be a regular management tool, not a one-time inspection. It helps owners understand whether systems, staff and records are working together effectively.

The strongest audits combine documentation review, physical verification, data analysis, staff observation and corrective follow-up.

Quick Summary

Key Takeaways

✓

Audit records, stock and real staff practices.

✓

Review inventory, expiry, purchasing and billing.

✓

Separate critical, major and minor findings.

✓

Assign every action to a responsible person.

✓

Verify that corrective actions solve the root cause.

✓

Repeat audits regularly to measure improvement.
