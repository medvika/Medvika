How to Get a Medical Store Licence in India: Complete Guide for 2026

Learn how to obtain a medical store licence in India in 2026, including retail and wholesale licences, pharmacist rules, premises, documents, inspection, fees and compliance.

Important: This guide provides general information, not legal advice. Drug-licensing procedures, document formats, fees and service timelines can differ by state and may change. Always confirm the latest requirements with the State Drug Control Department before paying fees, signing a long lease or purchasing inventory.

Opening a medical store is not the same as opening an ordinary retail shop. Medicines affect public health, so their sale, storage and dispensing are regulated. Before stocking or selling drugs, a pharmacy normally needs the appropriate drug sale licence from the licensing authority of the state or union territory in which the premises is located.

The central legal framework comes primarily from the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, the Drugs Rules, 1945, and the Pharmacy Act, 1948. State drug-control authorities administer sale licences, scrutinise applications and inspect premises. This means the basic legal structure is national, while the portal, document checklist, payment method and processing practice may vary by state.

This guide explains the process for an ordinary allopathic retail pharmacy and also highlights wholesale, Schedule X and state-specific considerations.

01

Quick answer: what is required to open a medical store?

For a typical retail pharmacy, an applicant should plan for:

  • A legally usable commercial premises, generally with at least 10 square metres of floor area for retail-only operations
  • A registered pharmacist who will personally supervise retail sale and dispensing
  • Appropriate storage, including a refrigerator or other controlled-temperature equipment where required for the products stocked
  • Proof of ownership or lawful tenancy and a clear site plan
  • Constitution documents for the proprietorship, partnership, LLP or company
  • Identity, qualification, registration and appointment documents for the pharmacist
  • An application to the relevant State Drug Control Department, normally using Form 19 for the common retail categories
  • Payment of the applicable government fee
  • Successful scrutiny and, where required, inspection of the premises
  • Ongoing compliance with prescription, purchase, sale, storage and record-keeping rules

Do not buy saleable medicine stock before confirming that the correct licence has been granted for the premises and product categories.

02

What is a drug licence?

A drug sale licence is the regulatory permission to sell, stock, exhibit, offer for sale or distribute specified categories of drugs from an approved premises. The licence is tied to important particulars such as the licensee, business constitution, premises, qualified person and permitted category of sale.

One licence does not automatically cover every activity. A retail pharmacy selling medicines to consumers has different permissions and conditions from a wholesale business supplying other dealers or institutions. Drugs in special categories can also require an additional licence.

03

Types of medical store licences

1. Retail drug licence

A retail drug licence is used when medicines are sold directly to patients or consumers. For a typical allopathic pharmacy, the commonly encountered licence forms are:

  • Form 20: Retail sale of drugs other than those specified in Schedules C, C(1) and X
  • Form 21: Retail sale of drugs specified in Schedules C and C(1), excluding Schedule X

Many full-service medical stores need both Form 20 and Form 21 permissions because their inventory can include ordinary medicines as well as products falling within Schedules C and C(1).

2. Wholesale drug licence

A wholesale licence is required when the business supplies medicines for resale or to eligible institutions rather than conducting ordinary consumer retail sales. Common forms include:

  • Form 20B: Wholesale sale of drugs other than those specified in Schedules C, C(1) and X
  • Form 21B: Wholesale sale of drugs specified in Schedules C and C(1), excluding Schedule X

Wholesale operations require a competent person who meets the qualification and experience conditions accepted by the licensing authority. This should not be confused with the registered-pharmacist requirement for retail dispensing.

3. Combined retail and wholesale licence

A business intending to conduct both activities should apply for the appropriate retail and wholesale permissions. This choice affects the premises area, qualified-person requirements, documentation and records.

4. Restricted retail licence

Restricted licences in Forms 20A and 21A relate to specified drugs whose sale does not require the supervision of a qualified person, subject to the limits and conditions of the Rules. This is not a substitute for a normal full-service pharmacy licence.

5. Schedule X licence

Schedule X drugs are subject to stricter controls. Retail sale is licensed separately, commonly in Form 20F, with enhanced prescription, custody and record requirements. A normal Form 20 or Form 21 licence does not by itself authorise Schedule X sales.

6. Separate systems of medicine

Ayurvedic, Siddha, Unani and homoeopathic products can fall under different provisions and state procedures. A business intending to deal exclusively or substantially in these categories should confirm the exact licensing route instead of assuming the allopathic retail process applies unchanged.

04

Does the medical store owner have to be a pharmacist?

Not necessarily. A proprietor, partner, director or investor may own the pharmacy business without personally holding a pharmacy qualification. However, retail sale and dispensing must be conducted under the personal supervision of a registered pharmacist, in accordance with the applicable law and licence conditions.

Section 42 of the Pharmacy Act restricts dispensing on the prescription of a medical practitioner to a registered pharmacist, subject to the Act's provisions. State authorities commonly require evidence of the pharmacist's qualification, current State Pharmacy Council registration, appointment and acceptance of the role.

The pharmacist should genuinely work at and supervise the licensed premises during pharmacy operations. Using a registration certificate only on paper while the pharmacist is absent can lead to objections, suspension or cancellation and exposes both the business and pharmacist to regulatory action.

If the pharmacist resigns or changes, notify the licensing authority promptly and follow the prescribed change-of-pharmacist procedure. Do not continue dispensing as if nothing changed.

05

Minimum area required for a medical store

The commonly applied minimum areas under Rule 64 are:

Proposed operation General minimum premises area
Retail only, Forms 20 and/or 21 10 m²
Wholesale only, Forms 20B and/or 21B 10 m²
Retail plus wholesale 15 m²

The area is only one part of premises suitability. The shop should be independent and suitable for safe storage and sale. Local land-use, municipal, building, fire-safety and commercial-use requirements may also apply. For example, Delhi's Department of Drugs Control specifically links premises acceptability to applicable land-use requirements and asks for supporting premises documents.

Before finalising a lease, verify:

  • The usable internal area shown in the site plan
  • Legal commercial or mixed-use permission, where applicable
  • Clear access and adequate height, ventilation and lighting
  • Reliable electricity for temperature-controlled storage
  • Whether the owner permits pharmaceutical retail activity
  • Whether the address and floor description match across all documents

A mismatch between the lease, electricity bill, site plan and application is a frequent and avoidable source of delay.

06

Storage and infrastructure requirements

Medicines must be stored under the conditions stated on their labels and required by the Rules. A proposed pharmacy should ordinarily arrange:

  • Clean racks, cabinets and storage away from direct sunlight, moisture and contamination
  • A refrigerator suitable for medicines requiring cold storage
  • Temperature monitoring and a written process for power failures
  • Segregated space for expired, damaged, recalled or returned products
  • Secure storage for specially controlled medicines, where applicable
  • A billing and inventory system capable of preserving required purchase and sale records
  • Pest control, housekeeping and stock-rotation procedures

A domestic refrigerator is sometimes presented during applications, but merely possessing one is not enough. The operator should be able to maintain the labelled temperature range, monitor it and act when temperatures move outside limits.

07

Documents commonly required

The exact checklist should be downloaded from the relevant state portal. A typical application may require the following.

Applicant and business documents

  • Applicant's photograph, identity proof, address proof, email address and mobile number
  • PAN and constitution proof of the business
  • Proprietorship declaration, partnership deed, LLP documents or certificate of incorporation and constitutional documents, as applicable
  • Authorisation or board resolution for the person signing on behalf of an entity
  • Affidavit or declaration regarding compliance and non-conviction, where prescribed
  • GST registration and local business registrations, if applicable at that stage

Premises documents

  • Registered or otherwise accepted sale deed if owned
  • Rent or lease agreement and rent receipt if rented
  • Ownership proof and no-objection document from the landlord, where required
  • Recent electricity bill, water bill or property-tax record as accepted by the authority
  • Site plan showing dimensions and total area
  • Proof of lawful commercial use or municipal conversion, where applicable
  • Refrigerator or cold-storage details and supporting purchase document, if requested

Registered pharmacist documents for retail

  • Degree or diploma qualification certificate and mark sheets
  • Valid registration certificate from the relevant State Pharmacy Council
  • Appointment letter and joining or acceptance documents
  • Identity and address proof, photographs and biodata
  • Affidavit or declaration of full-time engagement, where prescribed
  • Relieving letter or cancellation from a previous establishment, if the authority requires it

Competent-person documents for wholesale

  • Qualification certificates
  • Experience certificate meeting the applicable criteria
  • Appointment and acceptance documents
  • Identity, photographs, biodata and prescribed affidavit

Keep names, spellings, addresses and dates consistent across every upload. If the pharmacist's council registration is from another state, confirm whether transfer, reciprocal registration or registration with the local State Pharmacy Council is required before applying.

08

Step-by-step application process

Step 1: Decide the business activity

Define whether the outlet will be retail only, wholesale only, retail plus wholesale, or will handle Schedule X or another special category. Applying for the wrong category creates avoidable rework.

Step 2: Finalise the business constitution

Choose the legal structure and complete its core documents. The applicant name and constitution must remain consistent throughout the application, premises papers, tax records and bank arrangements.

Step 3: Verify the premises before signing a long lease

Measure the usable shop area and confirm land use, access, electricity and storage suitability. Obtain the owner's documents and written permission where required.

Step 4: Appoint the qualified person

For retail, appoint a pharmacist with valid registration acceptable in that state. For wholesale, appoint an eligible competent person. Confirm that the individual is not simultaneously attached to another establishment in a way that prevents genuine full-time supervision.

Step 5: Prepare the premises

Install racks, refrigerator, temperature monitoring and segregation arrangements. Display the proposed business name consistently. Do not stock medicines for sale before licensing.

Step 6: Create an account on the applicable portal

The application channel differs by jurisdiction. Uttar Pradesh routes business approvals through Nivesh Mitra and its Food Safety and Drug Administration service. Uttarakhand lists drug-sale licensing services through Invest Uttarakhand. Delhi provides its procedure and checklist through the Department of Drugs Control.

Step 7: Complete the prescribed application

For the usual retail or wholesale drug sale categories, Form 19 is commonly used, with the exact selections determining the licence forms requested. Complete every field carefully and upload clear, legible documents in the specified format.

Step 8: Pay the government fee

Pay only through the official portal or prescribed government channel. The payable amount depends on the state, licence combination and any additional category. Treat advertisements quoting one universal “drug licence cost” as estimates, not official fees.

Step 9: Respond to scrutiny

The authority may raise an online query about missing, inconsistent or unclear documents. Reply within the permitted time and replace defective documents rather than uploading unrelated explanations.

Step 10: Premises inspection

Where inspection is undertaken, the Drug Inspector may verify the premises, area, storage equipment, qualified person and supporting originals. The pharmacist or competent person should be present if directed.

Step 11: Download and verify the licence

After approval, check the business name, address, licence forms, qualified person's name and other conditions. Display the licence prominently as required and begin purchasing stock only from authorised suppliers against proper invoices.

09

Government fees and professional costs

There is no single reliable all-India total because applicants may need different combinations of licences and state services. The total setup outlay can include:

  • Statutory application or licence fees
  • Late or retention fees, if applicable later
  • Pharmacy Council-related cost for the pharmacist
  • Stamp duty, notarisation and document preparation
  • Site plan or professional certification charges
  • Municipal or local establishment permissions
  • Refrigerator, racks, temperature devices and power backup
  • Professional assistance, if voluntarily engaged

As one current state example, the Uttar Pradesh Nivesh Mitra approval list displays a government fee of ₹1,500 and a listed timeline of 15 days for its retail-licence service. Applicants should verify the live service page and final portal-generated amount because the selected licence combination and application circumstances may affect what is payable. Do not present this figure as the nationwide total cost of opening a medical store.

10

How long does a drug licence take?

The official service timeline differs by state. A clean application can still be delayed by:

  • Incorrect premises area or site plan
  • Land-use or tenancy-document issues
  • Pharmacy Council registration discrepancies
  • Pharmacist already associated with another outlet
  • Missing constitution or authorisation documents
  • Unclear scans, inconsistent names or address mismatch
  • Delayed response to a departmental query
  • Premises not ready at inspection

For practical planning, do not commit to an opening date based only on an informal estimate. Check the official state service timeline, submit a complete file and allow additional time for queries and inspection.

11

Is a drug licence permanent?

The current framework uses a retention-fee system for covered licences. Under the amended Rule 63, a sale licence can remain valid if the licensee deposits the prescribed retention fee before the end of every succeeding five-year period, unless the licence is suspended or cancelled. Delay can attract a monthly late fee for a limited period, after which non-payment can result in deemed cancellation.

This is why calling the licence simply “lifetime valid” is misleading. The business must pay retention fees on time and continuously satisfy its licence conditions. Record the due date in a compliance calendar well in advance.

12

Compliance after the licence is granted

A licence is the beginning of compliance, not the end. The operator should implement controls for:

Purchase and supplier verification

Purchase medicines only from duly licensed manufacturers or dealers and preserve purchase invoices. Verify supplier licence details before onboarding a new vendor.

Prescription controls

Schedule H, H1 and X medicines are not ordinary over-the-counter products. Follow the prescription, dispensing, warning-label and record requirements that apply to each category. Schedule H1 and Schedule X records require special attention and prescribed retention.

Registered-pharmacist supervision

Ensure the pharmacist is genuinely present and supervising retail dispensing. Maintain attendance and employment records. Report a change in qualified staff to the licensing authority promptly.

Storage and temperature

Record refrigerator temperatures, rotate stock using earliest-expiry-first principles and investigate temperature excursions. Never return questionable stock to saleable inventory without a documented decision.

Expiry, damage and recall controls

Identify near-expiry stock early. Quarantine expired, damaged and recalled items so they cannot be sold accidentally. Maintain return or disposal documentation.

Bills and records

Issue compliant cash or credit memos and retain purchase, sale and prescription-related records for the periods applicable to the product category. A pharmacy-management system can reduce errors, but the licence holder remains responsible for compliance.

Change management

Do not silently change the premises, constitution, ownership, pharmacist or competent person. Seek endorsement, submit notice or make a fresh application as required. Licence conditions can allow only a limited transition period following certain constitution changes.

13

State-specific starting points

Uttarakhand

Drug sale licensing services are listed through the Invest Uttarakhand single-window system under the Department of Medical Health and Family Welfare. Applicants in Dehradun, Haridwar or elsewhere in the state should select the exact retail, wholesale or retention service and download its current SOP and fee structure before submission.

Practical tip: confirm the district jurisdiction, local commercial-use status and whether the current service is fully online or requires any departmental follow-up.

Uttar Pradesh

Uttar Pradesh uses Nivesh Mitra for relevant Food Safety and Drug Administration approvals. The portal provides application, payment, tracking and certificate-download stages. Choose the drug service carefully; food licences and drug licences are separate permissions even though the department name includes both functions.

Practical tip: match the premises district and competent jurisdiction exactly, and rely on the live portal checklist for the final upload set.

Delhi NCR

For premises inside the National Capital Territory of Delhi, consult the Delhi Department of Drugs Control procedure. Its published guidance specifies the minimum areas, premises evidence, pharmacist documents and land-use considerations. For NCR locations in Uttar Pradesh or Haryana, apply to the authority of the state where the premises physically lies—not to Delhi merely because the business markets itself as being in Delhi NCR.

Practical tip: Delhi premises can attract additional scrutiny around lawful commercial or mixed use. Resolve that point before committing to the shop.

14

Common mistakes that delay or jeopardise approval

  1. Renting a shop that is too small for the proposed retail-plus-wholesale model
  2. Assuming the business owner must be the pharmacist—or treating a pharmacist as a certificate-only arrangement
  3. Applying with a pharmacist whose registration is inactive or unsuitable for the state
  4. Submitting a rent agreement without sufficient ownership or commercial-use evidence
  5. Showing different business names or addresses across documents
  6. Selecting only one licence form although the planned inventory needs another category
  7. Stocking medicines before the licence is granted
  8. Treating the refrigerator as a formality and keeping no temperature process
  9. Ignoring an online departmental query or inspection communication
  10. Forgetting the five-year retention-fee due date
  11. Continuing after a pharmacist leaves without completing the required regulatory process
  12. Selling prescription medicines without the applicable prescription and records
15

Frequently asked questions

Can I open a medical store without a pharmacy degree?

You may be able to own the business without being a pharmacist, but ordinary retail dispensing must be supervised by a registered pharmacist. Ownership and professional supervision are different legal roles.

Can I use another person's pharmacist certificate?

Only appointing a pharmacist on paper is not compliant. The registered pharmacist must genuinely undertake the required supervision at the licensed premises.

Is 10 m² enough for every pharmacy?

Ten square metres is the general minimum for retail-only Forms 20 and/or 21. A combined retail-and-wholesale operation generally requires at least 15 m². Local premises and building rules can impose additional constraints.

Do I need both Form 20 and Form 21?

Many normal pharmacies apply for both because the forms cover different drug categories. Your planned inventory should determine the exact combination.

Is GST registration the same as a drug licence?

No. GST is a tax registration. A drug sale licence is regulatory permission concerning medicines. One does not replace the other.

Can one licence cover multiple branches?

Generally, each premises requires the appropriate licence. Confirm the process for every branch with the state authority before opening or transferring stock.

Can I sell medicines online with a retail drug licence?

Do not assume an ordinary retail licence automatically resolves every issue connected with online ordering, interstate supply, prescription verification, delivery, data handling and platform operations. Obtain state-specific professional advice before launching online medicine sales.

Can I shift the medical store to another address?

A drug licence is premises-specific. Shifting should be handled through the prescribed approval or fresh-licence process before sales begin at the new location.

What happens if the pharmacist resigns?

Stop treating the premises as though supervised by that pharmacist. Notify the licensing authority and complete the applicable replacement process promptly.

Is the licence valid forever?

Covered licences remain valid only while the prescribed retention fee is paid at each five-year interval and all licence conditions continue to be met, unless suspended or cancelled.
